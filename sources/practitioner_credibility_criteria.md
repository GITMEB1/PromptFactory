# Practitioner Credibility Criteria

Apply these criteria before accepting practitioner claims into the evidence set.

## Positive Credibility Signals
- **Relevant track record**: history of work in the exact domain (maintainer role, production ownership, publications, talks with technical depth).
- **Identity continuity**: stable author identity across platforms and prior outputs.
- **Evidence quality**: includes code, data, logs, benchmark method, or reproducible steps.
- **Constraint disclosure**: clearly states environment, assumptions, and limits.
- **Calibration behavior**: distinguishes certainty vs hypothesis and updates when corrected.
- **Independent corroboration**: similar conclusions from other credible practitioners or official docs.
- **Timeliness**: recently updated and version-aware for the target stack.

## Disqualifiers (Hard)
- Fabricated or unverifiable identity.
- Plagiarized or AI-generated spam with no attributable evidence.
- Undisclosed financial conflict directly tied to recommendation.
- Repeated materially false claims without correction.
- Data/benchmark manipulation signals (missing method, cherry-picked comparisons, impossible metrics).

## Conditional Exclusions (Soft)
- Advice older than ecosystem change boundary (major version/architecture shift).
- Strong claims with no reproducibility details.
- Overgeneralized recommendations from niche context.
- Highly promotional content with weak technical depth.

## Acceptance Decision
A practitioner source should be included only if:
1. No hard disqualifier is present.
2. At least **three** positive signals are met, including evidence quality.
3. For medium/high-risk claims, at least one corroborating source exists outside the same author/channel.
