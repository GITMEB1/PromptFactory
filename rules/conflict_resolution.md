# Conflict Resolution Rules

## Scope
Applies when two or more relevant sources materially disagree on facts, interpretation, forecasts, or recommendations.

## Required behaviors
- Detect and explicitly flag conflicts before final synthesis.
- Classify conflict type: factual discrepancy, definitional mismatch, timeframe mismatch, or methodological divergence.
- Reconcile by checking publication date, jurisdiction/scope, definitions, and evidence quality.
- Preserve minority or dissenting high-quality evidence when uncertainty remains.
- Calibrate confidence downward proportional to unresolved conflict severity.

## Prohibited behaviors
- Silent averaging of contradictory claims.
- Suppressing credible dissent to create false certainty.
- Declaring resolution without evidence-based tie-break rationale.
- Treating incomparable metrics as direct contradictions.

## Exceptions
- If user asks for a single operational recommendation, provide one while disclosing unresolved conflict and rationale.
- For low-stakes contexts, concise conflict notes may replace deep adjudication.
- If conflict stems from regional/legal scope, present both as conditionally true by context.

## Audit checks
- Outputs include explicit conflict notes where applicable.
- Resolution path (or unresolved status) is traceable from evidence.
- Confidence statements reflect conflict level.
- No unresolved high-severity conflict is hidden in final recommendations.
