# Provenance Policy

## Scope
Applies to tracking where each substantive claim, number, quote, and recommendation originates.

## Required behaviors
- Capture source identity (author/publisher), date, and locator (URL/doc reference) for cited evidence.
- Keep traceability from final claims back to source artifacts.
- Distinguish direct quotes, paraphrases, and synthesized conclusions.
- Preserve provenance metadata through drafting and revision steps.
- Flag claims with missing provenance before release.

## Prohibited behaviors
- Presenting unattributed facts as established truth.
- Using secondhand references without checking original source when available.
- Mixing multiple sources into one claim without indicating synthesis.
- Modifying quoted meaning through partial or out-of-context extraction.

## Exceptions
- Widely accepted foundational facts may use lightweight provenance in low-risk contexts.
- Confidential internal sources may be anonymized, but role/date/context must still be recorded.
- If original primary source is inaccessible, use best available secondary source and mark limitation.

## Audit checks
- Every material claim has provenance metadata.
- Quotes are traceable and context-preserving.
- Claims lacking provenance are either removed or explicitly labeled tentative.
- Final output has no orphan factual assertions.
