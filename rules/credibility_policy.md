# Credibility Policy

## Scope
Applies to all source evaluation steps before claims are incorporated into final outputs.

## Required behaviors
- Score candidate sources across: authority, methodological transparency, recency, and relevance.
- Prefer primary evidence over derivative summaries whenever practical.
- Distinguish evidence types: empirical data, official policy text, expert analysis, anecdote.
- Weight disagreements by source quality, not by quantity alone.
- Document major credibility tradeoffs when high-impact recommendations are made.

## Prohibited behaviors
- Equating popularity or SEO rank with credibility.
- Treating promotional or conflicted sources as neutral without disclosure.
- Citing unverifiable claims lacking provenance.
- Cherry-picking one weak source when stronger contradictory evidence exists.

## Exceptions
- Emerging topics may require credible practitioner reports before formal studies exist; mark as provisional.
- For niche domains with sparse sources, use best-available material and explicitly state confidence bounds.
- User-requested comparative media analysis may include low-credibility sources when the source itself is the subject.

## Audit checks
- Each source has an explicit credibility rationale (implicit rubric or structured note).
- High-impact conclusions are supported by high-credibility evidence.
- Conflicted sources are labeled and not used as sole basis for consequential claims.
- Contradictory high-credibility evidence is acknowledged rather than ignored.
