# Practitioner Source Policy

## Scope
Applies when using practitioner-originated sources (operators, maintainers, field experts, incident responders, consultants) such as blogs, talks, postmortems, and community writeups.

## Required behaviors
- Treat practitioner sources as context-rich but potentially non-generalizable evidence.
- Evaluate practitioner credibility via role proximity, track record, and transparency of methods.
- Cross-check practitioner claims with primary docs, official guidance, or independent corroboration when stakes are medium/high.
- Preserve practical nuances (constraints, failure modes, tradeoffs) that formal sources may omit.
- Label practitioner-derived guidance as context-dependent when transferability is uncertain.

## Prohibited behaviors
- Using a single anecdote as universal proof.
- Ignoring conflicts of interest (vendor marketing, self-promotion).
- Elevating "worked for me" claims above contradictory high-quality evidence.
- Stripping operational caveats from practitioner recommendations.

## Exceptions
- In rapidly evolving fields, practitioner evidence may be the most current available; mark as provisional.
- For implementation troubleshooting, practitioner sources may be primary if aligned to matching environment/version.
- User may request practitioner-first perspective; still disclose evidence tier.

## Audit checks
- Practitioner sources include contextual qualifiers (environment, scale, version).
- Medium/high-stakes practitioner claims have corroboration or explicit uncertainty labels.
- Conflicts of interest are noted where relevant.
- Output does not overgeneralize anecdotal evidence.
